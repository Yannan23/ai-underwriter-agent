# Professional Indemnity Underwriting Guidelines (pi-guidelines.v1)

Miscellaneous professional risks, Australia-wide, written on a claims-made
basis. These guidelines define delegated underwriting authority for the
occupations in the appetite taxonomy. Anything not expressly within appetite
must be referred. Chunk ids are cited by the risk-assessment stage; sections
may be appended but never reordered within a version.

# Appetite

## Management Consultants

Management consultancy risks with annual fee income under $5,000,000 are core
appetite. Standard limit offerings are $1,000,000 and $2,000,000 any one
claim. Strategy consulting, operational reviews, organisational design, and
process-improvement engagements are acceptable at standard terms, including
work for government departments at the state or federal level provided the
engagement is advisory only.

Engagements that involve construction project management, owner's-side
project supervision, or certification of works fall outside the miscellaneous
appetite and must be referred, whatever the fee split. Advice that constitutes
financial product advice under the Corporations Act (for example advising on
mergers-and-acquisitions transactions, capital raising, or investment
selection) requires an Australian Financial Services Licence and is outside
delegated authority — refer any risk with more than an incidental exposure.

Success-fee or contingent-fee remuneration above 20% of annual fee income
warrants referral because it correlates with disputes over deliverables.
Sole practitioners with fewer than two years of consulting practice history
should be offered the $1,000,000 limit only, with the standard excess doubled,
unless they evidence five or more years in an equivalent salaried role.

## IT Consultants

Information-technology consulting — software development, systems integration,
business analysis, cloud migration, and managed services — is acceptable with
annual fee income under $5,000,000. The policy responds to third-party
financial loss from a negligent act, error, or omission in the professional
services; first-party cyber events and network-security liability are not
covered here and should be signposted to a cyber product rather than endorsed
onto the PI wording.

Refer any risk where deliverables are safety-critical or life-critical:
medical-device software, industrial control or SCADA systems, avionics, and
autonomous-vehicle components. Refer fintech engagements where the insured's
platform holds, moves, or reconciles client funds, and any development work
for gambling operators. Blockchain and cryptocurrency projects are outside
appetite entirely.

Contract review matters more for IT than any other class: watch for uncapped
liability for consequential loss, liquidated-damages clauses tied to
go-live dates, and fitness-for-purpose warranties. Where more than 30% of fee
income comes from fixed-price development with liquidated damages, apply a
25% loading or refer. Standard terms otherwise, with the $2,000,000 limit
available where written engagement terms cap liability.

## Accountants and Bookkeepers

Public-practice accountants deriving income from compilation of financial
statements, management accounting, taxation returns, and business-activity
statements are core appetite up to $5,000,000 in fees. Registered tax agents
and BAS agents in good standing with the Tax Practitioners Board qualify for
standard terms. Bookkeepers are the lowest-hazard occupation in the taxonomy
and typically present fee income under $500,000; write at the minimum premium
with the $1,000,000 limit.

The following activities are outside delegated authority and must be referred
however small the fee split: audit or review of listed or disclosing
entities, insolvency and formal restructuring appointments, and acting as
trustee. Financial planning or any personal financial product advice requires
an AFSL and is not written under this facility. Self-managed superannuation
fund work is acceptable only where it is administration and compliance;
refer SMSF investment advice.

Loadings apply where a single client contributes more than 40% of fee income
(concentration risk, 15%) or where the practice has grown by acquisition in
the last two years without file-review procedures (10%).

## Marketing, Media and Training Providers

Marketing and media consultancies — campaign strategy, brand development,
creative services, digital and social-media management — are acceptable up to
$5,000,000 fee income. The dominant claim sources are allegations of
defamation, infringement of intellectual-property rights, and campaigns that
misfire against agreed KPIs. Confirm the wording carries the defamation and
IP extensions; where the insured produces comparative advertising or
political campaign material, refer.

Training and education providers — corporate trainers, professional
development, compliance training — are acceptable at standard terms.
Registered Training Organisations (RTOs) delivering accredited qualifications
are acceptable provided current registration is confirmed; loss of
accreditation mid-course is the principal exposure, so decline any RTO
currently under ASQA review. Refer providers whose courses include high-risk
practical components (working at heights, confined spaces, driver training)
because the bodily-injury interface belongs in general liability, and refer
any provider guaranteeing employment or placement outcomes to students.

# Rating

## Claims History

Risks with no claims or notified circumstances in the past five years qualify
for standard terms. Each notified claim attracts a loading: 15% for the first
and 30% for each additional claim, applied to the technical premium before
minimum-premium comparison. Claims closed for nil payment where the insured's
file demonstrates sound engagement documentation may have the loading waived
at the underwriter's discretion, recorded in the decision log.

Any open claim, or a single claim exceeding $100,000 in paid or reserved
amounts, is outside delegated authority and must be referred with the claims
narrative, current reserve, and remediation steps. Three or more claims in
five years is decline territory for new business regardless of quantum —
frequency in a professional book is a stronger signal than severity.

Always distinguish claims from circumstances. A prudently notified
circumstance that never matured into a demand should not be rated as a claim,
but two or more notified circumstances in three years indicates weak
engagement hygiene: apply a 10% loading and require written engagement
letters as a condition of cover.

## Fee Income Banding

Base rates are expressed per band of annual fee income and multiplied by the
occupation base rate. Bands: up to $250,000 (factor 0.60); $250,001 to
$1,000,000 (factor 1.00); $1,000,001 to $2,500,000 (factor 1.35); $2,500,001
to $5,000,000 (factor 1.80). Fee income above $5,000,000 is outside the
facility cap and is a knockout at eligibility — do not attempt to rate it.

Fee income between $2,500,000 and $5,000,000 sits in the referral band: the
risk may proceed but requires senior-underwriter sign-off because larger
practices bring larger engagements and larger quantum. Use projected current-
year fee income where the business is growing more than 25% year on year;
otherwise use the last completed financial year. Where declared fee income is
materially inconsistent with staff count for the occupation (for example
$3,000,000 declared with two fee earners), query it — understatement of fees
is the most common form of premium leakage in this class.

Minimum premiums apply per occupation class and are not negotiable below
authority level.

## Limits and Excess Structure

Standard limits of indemnity are $1,000,000 and $2,000,000 any one claim,
with one automatic reinstatement. A $5,000,000 limit may be quoted only with
senior-underwriter approval and is reserved for clean risks with five-plus
years of continuous insurance. Limits above $5,000,000 are not available
under this facility.

Costs and expenses are payable in addition to the limit for limits up to
$2,000,000; the $5,000,000 limit is written costs-inclusive. The excess
applies to damages only, not defence costs, for all standard occupations.

Minimum excesses scale with fee income: $1,000 up to $250,000 of fees; $2,500
to $1,000,000; $5,000 to $2,500,000; $10,000 to $5,000,000. Insureds may
select a voluntary higher excess for a premium credit of up to 15%. Do not
offer excesses below the scale minimum: low excesses in this class generate
attritional notifications that cost more to handle than they transfer in
risk. Where contract hygiene is poor (see Conditions), impose the next excess
tier up rather than declining outright.

# Exposure

## Overseas Work and North American Revenue

Revenue derived from the United States or Canada above 10% of annual fee
income is outside appetite due to the litigation environment, jury awards,
and the cost of defending proceedings in those jurisdictions — knock out at
eligibility rather than attempting terms. US or Canadian revenue at or below
10% is acceptable provided the engagement is performed from Australia and
governed by Australian law; apply a 10% loading where it exceeds 5%.

Overseas exposure up to 10% of fee income in other common-law jurisdictions
(United Kingdom, New Zealand, Singapore, Hong Kong) is acceptable at standard
terms with worldwide territorial limits excluding US/Canada jurisdiction.
Exposure between 10% and 25% in those jurisdictions warrants referral with
detail of the governing law of the relevant contracts. Work performed under
the law of a non-common-law jurisdiction should be referred whatever the
percentage, because the wording's defence provisions assume adversarial
proceedings.

Ask where the work is performed, where the client is domiciled, and which law
governs the engagement — the three questions produce different answers more
often than proposers expect, and the governing law is the one that matters.

## Staff Count and Supervision

Supervision structure is a leading indicator of claims frequency. Require at
least one qualified principal (director or equivalent) for every ten fee
earners; refer any risk outside that ratio. For accountants, the qualified
principal must hold a current CA or CPA practising certificate; for RTO
trainers, current TAE qualifications.

Subcontractors and labour-hire consultants performing professional services
under the insured's brand are treated as staff for rating. Where
subcontracted work exceeds 30% of annual fee income, refer, and require
evidence that subcontractors carry their own professional indemnity insurance
with limits not less than $1,000,000 — the insured's policy should sit excess
of, not in place of, the subcontractor's own cover.

Headcount growth above 50% year on year strains file-review and peer-review
processes; apply a 10% loading and ask what quality-assurance procedures were
added alongside the hiring. Practices with fewer than two fee earners and fee
income above $1,000,000 should be queried for undeclared subcontracting.

# Cover

## Retroactive Dates

Cover operates on a claims-made basis: the policy responds to claims first
made and notified during the period of insurance, for acts committed after
the retroactive date. The retroactive date should align with the inception of
continuous prior professional indemnity insurance — request certificates of
currency or schedules evidencing the chain.

Unlimited retroactive cover may only be offered where five or more years of
continuous insurance and a clean claims record are both evidenced. Where
there has been any gap in cover, set the retroactive date at the inception of
the current continuous run — never backdate across a gap, because the gap is
exactly where an unreported circumstance lives.

New ventures with no prior insurance take a retroactive date at inception of
this policy. Where the proposer previously practised through a different
entity, the retroactive date does not travel with the individuals: prior-
entity work needs run-off cover from the prior insurer, and offering to pick
it up is outside delegated authority. Refer any request to backdate a
retroactive date more than twelve months earlier than the evidenced
continuous-insurance chain supports.

# Conditions

## Contract Hygiene and Engagement Letters

Consultants using written engagement letters with liability caps qualify for
the standard excess. The letter should define scope, deliverables, fees, an
exclusion of consequential loss, and a cap on aggregate liability — a cap at
the policy limit or a multiple of fees is acceptable market practice. Absence
of written engagement terms warrants an increased excess (next tier up) or
referral where combined with any other adverse feature.

Where the insured contracts on client paper, look for indemnities in favour
of the client, warranties of fitness for purpose, and any clause contracting
out of proportionate liability legislation — contracting out converts several
liability into joint and several liability and materially increases severity.
A risk that routinely accepts uncapped liability under client terms should be
referred with examples of its two largest current contracts.

Recommended conditions available to the underwriter: mandatory written
engagement letters within 30 days, an increased excess for claims arising
from engagements without written terms, and exclusion of specific named
projects. Conditions must be expressed so compliance is verifiable at claim
time — vague "best practice" conditions are unenforceable and should not be
used.
